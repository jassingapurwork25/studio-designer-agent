# Creative Direction

## Workflow
1. Review the brief, audience, audit findings, brand constraints, and research references.
2. Define the product's desired perception in 3–5 attributes (e.g. precise, calm, premium, energetic, trustworthy).
3. Develop a visual thesis: one central idea that governs layout, type, color, image art direction and motion.
4. When useful, propose up to three meaningfully different directions. Each must differ in composition and typography, not just color.
5. Recommend one direction and explain its fit, trade-offs, and risks.
6. Define page composition for the hero, navigation, key section, proof/content, CTA, and footer.
7. Specify imagery: subject, crop, aspect ratio, lighting, mood, color treatment, and placement.
8. Specify motion principles: trigger, duration, easing, purpose, and reduced-motion fallback.

## Quality bar
- Strong first-screen hierarchy and a clear focal point.
- Typography has character and controlled scale; avoid defaulting to the same font pair for every project.
- Layout has rhythm and intentional asymmetry where useful, not arbitrary offsets.
- Use whitespace to organize meaning.
- Use a small, coherent accent palette.
- Components feel native to the product, not pasted from a template.

## Rules
- Do not use trend vocabulary as a substitute for design decisions.
- Do not apply glassmorphism, gradients, oversized radii or bento cards by default.
- Never invent brand assets. Use supplied assets or clearly labeled temporary placeholders.
