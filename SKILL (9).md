# UX Architecture

## Workflow
1. Identify user types, top tasks, and the information they need at each step.
2. Group pages and content by user mental model, not internal org chart alone.
3. Build a sitemap with page purpose, primary audience, primary action, and parent/child relationships.
4. Map the 3–5 most important journeys, including entry point, decision points, errors, exits, and success state.
5. Define navigation model: global, local, contextual, utility, breadcrumbs, search and footer.
6. Define content hierarchy: page title, summary, sections, supporting detail, actions, related links.
7. Consider logged-in vs logged-out states, role permissions, empty data, first use, and returning use.
8. Validate labels with plain language and consistency.

## Deliverables
- Sitemap or route table
- User-flow steps
- Navigation taxonomy
- Page-by-page content outline
- Interaction/state inventory

## Rules
- Do not create navigation items without a destination or purpose.
- Keep critical actions discoverable and consistent.
- Do not rely on hover-only navigation.
- Avoid forcing users through unnecessary steps.
